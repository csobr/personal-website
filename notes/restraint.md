![A cyanotype-toned Dutch still life — candle, fruit bowl, game bird and lobster on a draped table — overrun with tangled cables, a laptop, a tablet and a phone.](/images/notes/constraintandrestraint.webp)

A kid in the Global South picks up an iPhone and shoots a short film for YouTube. No crew, no budget, no gear. Just the phone. A younger brother bouncing window light off a bedsheet. The result is beautifully composed. The single camera forced her to think harder about framing. With only a brother as crew, every shot had to be simple enough to pull off before he got impatient. The limitations didn't diminish the work. They shaped it.

Constraints force you off the obvious path. That's when you find the unexpected.

In the material world, limits are imposed for you. Cost, access, resources. These force prioritization. Use them. Let scarcity sharpen your craft. The kid with the iPhone didn't choose restraint. Her environment did. She just made the film.

In the digital world, creation is cheap and options infinite. With GenAI, for example, a one-sentence prompt to an LLM scatters through latent space like a drop in the ocean. What's returned is just as diffuse and general. Generous limits with a $200 plan mean vague prompts costs you your precious time. That's precisely when you push beyond the defaults. You get specific, you iterate, you learn what the model responds to.

But the deeper discipline is knowing when to stop, when to edit, when to choose. There's no need to share four variations. Pick the best one. Leave the rest.

Restraint is having options and choosing not to use them all. Not because you must, but because you refuse to settle.

Curiosity pulls you forward. Taste is never quite satisfied.

The friction that once kept excess in check has dissolved. The discipline that scarcity once provided must now come from within.

When options are endless, the work is editing and choosing. When options are few, the work is invention. Neither comes naturally. Both require practice.

Wishing you a more restrained 2026.

— Siham
