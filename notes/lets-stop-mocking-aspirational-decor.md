I came across a TikTok about interiors that “gives me instant depression.” I liked it instantly and the comments had me in stitches: “Jysk” (6k likes), “Come Dine With Me chic” (+11k likes). A very Euro taxonomy, but you know the style: beige leather, oak-effect tables, butter yellow painted walls, black and chrome details.

It got me thinking: why does this provoke such specific contempt? Many comments mention 2008, but the problem isn’t the year. It’s aspirational decor, then and now. Today we just have more grey variants.

And grey is more forgiving. Grey is like an all-black outfit: it makes no claim, so there’s nothing to mock. **Blandness is protected by its boringness.**

But sitting with it, I started to wonder what grey offers instead. Nothing. Safety. Silence.

Aspirational decor makes choices. Oak-effect tables, leather-look chairs, gold curtains. It reaches for something. You can see what they wanted. And that’s what gets punished. Visible aspiration.

Grey is meek, and that’s kinda off putting. There’s something charming about visible aspiration. Something I hadn’t considered before.

Nouveau riche with their shiny gold get mocked too, but at least they have resources. They can graduate from the shininess. The Jysk dining set is there to stay. When it falls apart, it’s back to flatpack land.

I respect the aspiration. It deserved a better chance. The answer isn’t grey, and ditching flatpack land isn’t an option. But it’s not this either. There’s a space between bland and embarrassing. That’s worth finding.

— Siham
